The problem

Before a company can pay a new supplier, someone has to check a stack of documents

In Saudi Arabia that means the commercial registration, the VAT certificate, a bank letter, the GOSI and Nitaqat certificate, and often more. Today it usually looks like this:

  1. The supplier emails their documents. Some are scans, some are in Arabic, the file names say nothing.
  2. Someone opens every PDF and copies the numbers into a spreadsheet.
  3. They check each company by hand on the Wathq and ZATCA websites.
  4. One certificate has expired, so they email the supplier and wait. Then again for the next problem.
  5. Weeks later the supplier is set up, and nobody watches their documents expire after that.

Big companies buy SAP Ariba or Coupa and make suppliers fill in portals. Most Saudi companies still do all of this by email and spreadsheet, and global tools don't check Saudi registries or read Arabic documents. So we built an agent that works where the work already happens: the inbox.

How it works

The work is split so that each part does what it is good at. The AI reads messy documents; plain code does the checks that must be exact; the agent decides what to do next.

The AI reads

Documents in, facts out

Scanned or digital, Arabic or English, Hijri or Gregorian dates. Each PDF becomes the same short list of facts: company name, CR number, IBAN, expiry date.

Code checks

Rules that can't be talked around

Is anything expired or missing? Is the CR active in Wathq? Does the bank account belong to the same company? Is the IBAN valid? The rules live in one settings file.

The agent acts

Fix, finish, or stop

Routine problem: it emails the supplier for exactly the missing document. All clear: it creates the vendor. Risk: it stops and asks you, with the evidence.

It never changes bank details on its own. Any email asking to move a supplier's payments to a new account is frozen until a person calls the supplier on the phone number already on file. This is the most common way companies lose money to email fraud.
